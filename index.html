Ball Mastery

Three simple activities to establish touch, control and confidence before the finishing work.

Each activity: 1 minute × 2 reps Rep 1 = control and clean touches Rep 2 = increase tempo without losing the ball
1

3-Cone Dribble Variations

Move through three cones using any combination of inside, outside and drag touches.

  • Keep the ball within playing distance.
  • Use both feet.
  • Change direction at each cone.

1 MIN × 2 REPS

Cue: Small touches → head up → stay balanced.

2

Inside / Outside Touches

Manipulate the ball around the cones using both the inside and outside of the foot.

  • Stay light on the feet.
  • Keep touches short and controlled.
  • Alternate feet whenever possible.

1 MIN × 2 REPS

Cue: Control first. Speed second.

3

Drags + Change of Direction

Use sole drags and direction changes to move the ball away from imaginary pressure.

  • Move the body with the ball.
  • Plant strongly before changing direction.
  • Exit with a purposeful touch.

1 MIN × 2 REPS

Cue: Drag → turn → accelerate out.

2

Skills → Exit → Shot

Each move follows the same pattern: learn it cleanly first, then immediately connect the move to a finish.

2 MINUTESPractice the move only — no shot.
→
4 MINUTESUse the same move, accelerate out, then shoot.
1

Step Over

Sell the defender one way with the body, step around the ball, then exit into the opposite space.

Cue: Sell the move → push out → accelerate.

2

Change Direction

Attack one side, use a sharp plant and redirect the ball into the open lane.

Cue: Sharp plant → clean exit touch.

3

La Croqueta

Transfer the ball quickly across the body to escape the defender's reach.

Cue: Two quick touches → explode away.

4

Step Over + Chop

Use the step over to freeze the defender, then chop across the body and leave through the new lane.

Cue: Freeze → chop → go.

When the shot is added: Approach the cone/defender → execute the skill → explosive exit touch → head up → finish.
3

Final 10 Minutes — Shooting Technique

Finish the session by correcting the player's biggest shooting need. Pick only one or two technical points so the player can actually feel the change.

10-Minute Structure

First 3 minutes — technical correction: slow to medium speed. Focus almost entirely on the selected mechanic.

Next 4 minutes — normal-speed finishing: use a moving ball or setup touch while keeping the same technical cue.

Final 3 minutes — game-speed finishing: receive, move and finish naturally. Check whether the correction still holds at speed.

Cue: Fix one thing at a time. Clean technique first, then add speed.

1. Plant Foot

Place the support foot beside the ball, stable and generally pointed toward the target.

Strong base before the strike.

2. Chest Over the Ball

Keep the chest slightly forward for a driven shot. Avoid leaning backward unless intentionally lifting the ball.

Stay over it for a driven strike.

3. Approach Step

Use a controlled final stride into the ball rather than reaching awkwardly at contact.

Smooth approach. Balanced final step.

4. Arm Action

The opposite arm helps balance and rotation. Keep the arms active and relaxed rather than stiff.

Let the arms support the strike.

5. Landing

Strike through the ball and allow momentum to carry the body forward. A natural landing on the shooting foot can show a complete follow-through.

Finish moving through the ball.

6. Locked Ankle / Clean Contact

Keep the ankle firm so the foot presents a strong, consistent contact surface.

Firm ankle. Clean strike.

7. Head + Eyes

See the target before the strike, then return the eyes to the ball and stay focused through contact.

Scan first. Eyes back to ball.
